## Approach
Our driver uses pure pursuit to follow the organiser-provided centreline. At
each LiDAR update it finds the nearest point on the closed racing path, chooses
a lookahead point, and publishes a normalised steering command to
/autodrive/roboracer_1/steering_command. The lookahead distance grows with
speed for stable tracking on straights. When the preview detects the 180-degree
hairpin, the lookahead distance is smoothly reduced so that the car can follow
the inside of the turn without a sudden steering jump.
The speed target is planned from three path situations: the straightaway speed
ceiling (v_target), a lower speed for normal bends (v_corner), and a lower
speed for the hairpin (v_hairpin). The target is blended smoothly between
these values. The driver also checks a narrow LiDAR sector aligned with the
steering direction and limits speed when an obstacle is very close. Throttle is
then generated by the shipped SpeedController, using feed-forward and PI
feedback. Steering and throttle are clipped to the permitted command ranges.
An earlier constant-speed approach and wider reactive steering were unsuitable
because they slowed the car unnecessarily or made it approach tight sections
too quickly. We retained the organiser’s vehicle geometry, topics, QoS profile,
and centreline path, and tuned the speed profile and lookahead behaviour in the
simulator.

## How it uses the inputs
- LiDAR: Reads /autodrive/roboracer_1/lidar. The scan is converted to
finite ranges and used for a narrow forward safety check aligned with the
current steering direction. It does not build a map.
- Pose: Reads /autodrive/roboracer_1/odom. The driver uses the simulator’s
ground-truth position and orientation to follow the supplied centreline; it
does not run its own localisation or SLAM.
- Camera / IMU / encoders: Not used.

## Speed control
The path preview produces a desired speed. The driver interpolates between the
straight, normal-corner, and hairpin targets, then sends that target and the
measured odometry speed to the shipped SpeedController. The controller uses
feed-forward plus proportional and integral feedback to produce the
throttle_command. A steering-dependent throttle cap reduces drive torque when
the wheels are turned sharply. The final command is clipped to the simulator’s
allowed range.
The values in driver_params.yaml are read when the node starts. The final
submitted values must be recorded here from the exact YAML used for the judged
run, especially v_target, v_corner, v_hairpin, and throttle_max.

## Anything precomputed
The driver uses the organiser-provided icra26_compete_centerline.csv path.
We did not train a model, generate an occupancy grid, or create an offline map.
The path is loaded at startup and used by pure pursuit and the local bend
preview. No additional precomputed asset is required.

## Known issuesThe driver depends on the organiser-provided centreline and the simulator’s
ground-truth odometry, so it is not a general localisation or mapping system.
The speed values are tuned assumptions for this vehicle and circuit; a higher
speed can increase the risk of leaving the track or colliding. The narrow LiDAR
sector is intended for emergency protection and does not perform full vehicle
footprint collision checking or obstacle replanning. The speed controller and
helper modules should be tested from a clean clone before submission.
